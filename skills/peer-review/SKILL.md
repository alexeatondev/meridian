# Skill: peer-review

Review exactly two peers: one from your family (technical errors within a shared framework) and one from another family
(challenge assumptions). For each: strengths, weaknesses, IPS-fit, estimation risk, one concrete improvement.
Voting lenses: heuristic → lowest tracking error; return-optimized → highest expected Sharpe; risk-structured → lowest volatility and highest effective N;
adversarial → furthest from consensus. Top-3 ballot (3/2/1) + bottom flag (−2); never vote for yourself. The lens is where you start, not where you stop: all reviews
are released before the vote, so weigh what your peers and the risk officer found.
Scores run 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). A method that is orthogonal by design (the adversarial diversifier) should be judged on
whether it adds diversification to an ensemble, not on whether it would stand alone.
Final ranking blends votes with a metric composite (Sharpe 30%, TE 25%, drawdown 20%, effective N 15%, compliance 10%);
vote weight 0.4 in late-cycle/recession, 0.5 otherwise.
